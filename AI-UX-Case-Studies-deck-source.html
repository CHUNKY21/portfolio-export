Meridian Research Agent
A conversational research agent for investment operations
Analysts used to manually cross-reference five-plus internal systems to investigate a discrepancy. The agent now takes a plain-language question, retrieves from connected sources, and returns a cited, confidence-scored answer.
“I'd have four systems open just to answer one question — and I still wasn't sure I had the full picture.”
illustrative — not a verbatim quote
Introduction    Meridian Research Agent
What’s Meridian Research Agent?
Analysts used to manually cross-reference five-plus internal systems to investigate a discrepancy. The agent now takes a plain-language question, retrieves from connected sources, and returns a cited, confidence-scored answer.
Target audience
Investment operations analyst
The problems
  • Manually opening 4-5 systems for a single research question
  • No consistent starting point — every analyst improvises their own process
  • No way to show your work after the fact — answers aren't auditable
  • Repetitive, low-judgment lookups take the same time as genuinely hard investigations
Led the design process for this concept, from discovery interviews through the shipped v1 experience.

Design / Process    Meridian Research Agent
The 8-Stage AI-Enhanced UX Process
Discovery & Research — Real interviews with analysts and team lead. New: mapped what data sources could answer with confidence vs. requiring human judgment.
Problem Definition — Synthesized the four pain points. New: separated ‘slow workflow’ from ‘unverifiable output’ — only the second needs interaction design, not just a feature.
Competitive / Pattern Analysis — Benchmarked adjacent AI research tools for how they handle uncertainty. Set the bar: ‘the citation is part of the answer,’ not optional.
Design Thinking & Ideation — Centered on agent behavior before any screen: uncertainty, correction, and the pull-vs-handoff line.
Design Sprints & Iteration — Real, iterative sprints with weekly usability testing.
Usability Testing — Tested happy and failure paths equally; watched whether analysts verified sources unprompted.
Delivered Solution — Real, shipped v1 — query input, cited answer, confidence signal, show-your-work expand, fallback state.
Outcomes — See Results — one real sourced metric, three illustrative estimates.
What we learned
Early versions surfaced confidence only when the agent flagged low confidence itself. Testing showed analysts trusted the agent more — not less — when confidence was shown on every answer.

Meridian Exception Manager
From a flat queue to a ranked worklist the team trusts
An AI layer that detects, prioritizes, and proposes remediation for operational exceptions inside Meridian — presenting them as a ranked, actionable worklist instead of a flat queue.
“Every exception looked the same until I opened it. I had no way to know what actually mattered.”
illustrative — not a verbatim quote
Introduction    Meridian Exception Manager
What’s Meridian Exception Manager?
An AI layer that detects, prioritizes, and proposes remediation for operational exceptions inside Meridian — presenting them as a ranked, actionable worklist instead of a flat queue.
Target audience
Operations analyst (triage mode) · Team lead
The problems
  • Exceptions arrived as a flat queue with no reliable signal for what mattered
  • Prioritization depended on tribal knowledge held by experienced analysts
  • High-risk exceptions could sit unworked for hours, indistinguishable from routine ones
Led the design process for this concept, from contextual interviews with the manual-triage team through shipped v1.
Discovery & Research    Meridian Exception Manager
Why does this matter?
~35%
reduction in exception time-to-resolution (illustrative target)
Discovery methods: contextual interviews with operations analysts doing manual triage, plus their team lead, capturing the implicit rules experienced staff already use
Problem statement: operational exceptions surfaced through queues and manual triage, so prioritization depended on tribal knowledge of ‘which ones actually matter’

Design / Process    Meridian Exception Manager
The 8-Stage AI-Enhanced UX Process
Discovery & Research — Contextual interviews with the triage team. New: mapped what the system could rank with confidence vs. needing human judgment, and the trust baseline toward being told vs. asked.
Problem Definition — Synthesized triage pain points. New: separated ‘unranked queue’ from ‘I don't trust being interrupted by a flag.’
Competitive / Pattern Analysis — Benchmarked proactive alerting tools on how they handle being wrong when pushing something — unexplained alerts get ignored within weeks.
Design Thinking & Ideation — Centered on the moment the system interrupts someone: low-confidence behavior, post-dismissal state, detect-vs-confirm boundary.
Design Sprints & Iteration — Earliest version ranked with a bare numeric score; testing showed distrust as ‘another black box’ — replaced with a plain-language reason.
Usability Testing — Tested whether analysts worked the list top-to-bottom (trust) or re-sorted it themselves (distrust signal); tested over-flagging deliberately.
Delivered Solution — Shipped v1 — continuous detection and ranking, visible reason + proposed remediation, AI-proposed vs. human-confirmed states, first-class dismissal.
Outcomes — See Results — illustrative targets; no sourced measured figure exists for this concept.
Unexpected challenge
The riskiest early choice wasn't the ranking algorithm — it was a bare priority score with no reason. Testing showed an unexplained pushed number is trusted less than a flat, unranked queue.

AI Pattern Library
A design system for AI states
A defined set of reusable AI-interaction components — confidence, sourcing, in-progress response, correction, human handoff — documented and governed the same way as any other design-system component.
“We built confidence three different ways across three teams, and none of them talk to each other.”
illustrative, constructed for this case study
Introduction    AI Pattern Library
What’s AI Pattern Library?
A defined set of reusable AI-interaction components — confidence, sourcing, in-progress response, correction, human handoff — documented and governed the same way as any other design-system component.
Target audience
Product designers across multiple teams shipping AI features
The problems
  • Every team invents its own confidence visualization, no shared language
  • Citation/sourcing shown inconsistently across teams
  • No shared ‘still generating’ affordance — finished or stalled?
Led the design process, drawing on real design-system governance leadership at Wells Fargo, Credit Suisse, and BlackRock; this specific engagement is constructed for the case study.

Problem statement: standard design systems don't have components for AI-specific states — no established pattern for sourcing, confidence, in-progress response, or correction

  5. No consistent human-handoff path

Design / Process    AI Pattern Library
The 8-Stage AI-Enhanced UX Process
Discovery & Research — Organizational audit + stakeholder interviews. New: mapped what's reusable across any AI feature vs. product-specific.
Problem Definition — Synthesized the audit. New: separated ‘pattern doesn't exist’ (design system fixes) from ‘users don't trust AI output’ (only component interaction design fixes).
Competitive / Pattern Analysis — The ‘competitor’ is internal — every team's ad hoc solution — benchmarked against published external AI-design guidance.
Design Thinking & Ideation — Defined each component's behavior independent of any one product's implementation; governance treated as part of ideation.
Design Sprints & Iteration — Same cadence as prior non-AI design-system work, iterating on components whose underlying data is non-deterministic.
Usability Testing — Tested trust/comprehension at the component level AND adoption at the governance level — two layers a normal rollout doesn't need.
Delivered Solution — v1 ships 5 documented components, each with an explicit boundary between what the pattern owns and what stays product-specific.
Outcomes — See Results — targets, not measured results.
What we learned
The confidence indicator's first draft used red/yellow/green — pilot review found red collided with the org's error-color language. Redesigned around a neutral intensity scale plus a plain-text qualifier.

Introduction    Compliance Co-Pilot
What’s Compliance Co-Pilot?
A concept where a research agent, a policy-check agent, and an escalation agent handle parts of submission review — with every action visible on a timestamped trail.
Target audience
Risk & Compliance manager · Submitter/Employee
The problems
  • Submissions returned for missing information, with no clear explanation
  • No visibility into where a submission is in the review pipeline or why
  • No consolidated view of a submitter's disclosure history for judgment calls
