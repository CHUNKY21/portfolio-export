# Epic 5 — Connected Vehicle Assistant
*(Real client: American Honda — multi-agent concept, adds a second industry to the portfolio)*

Part of the AI UX portfolio (5 case studies). See `../case-study-requirements.md` for the index of all five, `../case-study-concepts.md` for the original pitch, and `../ai-ux-process-framework.md` for the shared Who/What/When/Why/How template and stage framework this follows.

---

**WHO**
- Primary persona: vehicle owner using a connected-vehicle mobile app or in-vehicle interface
- Secondary: dealer/service advisor who receives service recommendations generated by the assistant
- Stakeholders: product/engineering teams owning vehicle telematics data, dealer network operations

**WHAT**
- Problem: owners get fragmented, one-off notifications (a maintenance reminder here, a diagnostic alert there) with no unified assistant tying scheduling, diagnostics, and service recommendations together
- Proposed solution (concept): a multi-agent assistant — a scheduling agent, a diagnostics agent, and a service-recommendation agent — that coordinate to give the owner one coherent assistant experience instead of three disconnected notification sources

**WHEN**
- Ambient/ongoing (diagnostics agent watches vehicle health continuously) combined with pull moments (owner asks "when's my next service" or "what does this warning light mean")
- Framed as concept/vision work extending your real American Honda service-design research

**WHY**
- Business goal: increase service-scheduling conversion and reduce missed-maintenance-driven issues; strengthen the owner-to-dealer relationship
- User goal: understand what's happening with their vehicle without needing to interpret disconnected alerts themselves
- Success metrics (concept-stage hypotheses): service-scheduling conversion rate from an assistant-initiated prompt, reduction in "what does this alert mean" service-center calls, owner trust/satisfaction rating

**HOW**
- Design approach: extend your real Honda customer-interview and journey-mapping work into a concept assistant — reference the actual pain points and service blueprints from that engagement as the foundation
- Core design challenge: same legibility problem as Epic 4 — when three agents can all surface something to the owner, the UX has to make clear which one is talking and why, and avoid notification pile-up/fatigue
- Key interaction pattern: a unified assistant thread/feed where each agent's contribution is labeled (diagnostic vs. scheduling vs. recommendation), rather than three separate notification streams

**User story**
> As a vehicle owner, I want one assistant that understands my vehicle's health, my schedule, and recommended service, so that I don't have to piece together disconnected alerts and reminders myself.

**Acceptance criteria (draft)**
- Owner sees a single unified feed/thread, with each entry labeled by which agent generated it
- Diagnostics-driven alerts always include plain-language explanation, not just a code/warning label
- Scheduling suggestions are actionable in one step (not a handoff to a separate app/flow)
- Clearly labeled as a concept/vision extension of real Honda research, not a shipped feature

**Out of scope / open questions**
- Not designing the underlying telematics/diagnostics system
- Open: in-vehicle interface, mobile app, or both — recommend picking one to keep the case study focused
