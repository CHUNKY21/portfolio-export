Connected Vehicle Assistant

A concept where a scheduling agent, a diagnostics agent, and a service-recommendation agent coordinate behind a single owner-facing assistant — with every entry labeled by which agent generated it.

"I get an alert, a reminder, and a text — and none of them talk to each other, so I don't know what actually matters."
(illustrative — not a verbatim quote)

Introduction

What's the Connected Vehicle Assistant?

A concept extending Michael's real American Honda service-design research: three specialized agents handle diagnostics, scheduling, and service recommendations, feeding one unified assistant feed instead of three disconnected notification sources.

Target audience
Vehicle owner, connected-vehicle mobile app

The problem

  1. Owners receive fragmented, one-off notifications — a maintenance reminder here, a diagnostic alert there — with no unified assistant tying scheduling, diagnostics, and service recommendations together

Extends design and discovery work from Michael's real American Honda service-design engagement into this forward-looking multi-agent concept.

Design / Process

Discovery & Research — real customer interviews and journey mapping carried over from prior Honda engagement. New: mapped which parts of the ownership experience are agent-automatable vs. requiring human judgment, and surfaced the owner's distinct trust baseline toward diagnostic claims.

Design Thinking & Ideation — centered on agent behavior: what each agent surfaces, how related outputs are merged rather than piled up, where agent-proposes/owner-confirms sits.

Design Sprints & Iteration / Usability Testing — not yet run; this is a concept/vision piece, no fabricated test results are presented.

Unexpected challenge

The hardest open question isn't individual agent accuracy — it's whether three independently-triggering agents actually reduce notification volume, or just relabel the same volume with better attribution.

Results (targets, not measured)

~20%
scheduling conversion uplift (target)
~35%

≥75%

Key Insight

"When three agents can each independently decide to reach the owner, the UX problem isn't whether each agent works — it's whether the owner ends up with one coherent assistant, or three notification sources wearing one label. Entry consolidation and per-entry agent attribution are the concept's actual answer to that risk."
